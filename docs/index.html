Byproduct experiment. Final summary of versions 1 to 3.

All three surviving theories say intelligence was a byproduct. A faithful simulation therefore scores agents only on staying alive and being copied, then measures general ability separately on held-out tests.

The prediction did not hold at this scale. In every version, selecting on survival alone raised skill on the tasks agents were selected on. General ability on held-out tests never moved beyond noise, and no theory arm beat plain selection.

The strongest form of the test was not run: the five social arms with changing weights, on a model large enough to pass the ceiling check.

The same pattern twice

Skill on the selected task sits 3.5 to 3.8 standard errors above chance in both versions. General ability sits inside the band in both. The version 2 paired check was added after seeing the data, so its pre-set outcome stays as "not supported".

Evidence for and against the thesis

For

  • Skill rose with no reward for skill. Version 2: SOLO gained +10.8 energy against +2.6 under chance. Version 3: SOLO ended at 29.7 against 21.3.
  • The mechanisms two theories need did operate in version 2. With an exile vote the grab rate was about 2% against about 8% without it. Inherited notes added +3.1 energy, higher in 5 of 5 seeds.
  • In version 1, ability rose in both solo-foraging arms in 5 of 5 seeds (SOLO +0.102, CULTURE +0.105). Both are inside the noise band of 0.175.
  • Breadth of pressure matters a little. Training on 16 task families lifted families never trained on by +0.054 (SE 0.016) over training on 3.

Against

  • General ability never moved beyond noise. Version 1: best arm +0.105 against a band of 0.175. Version 2: -0.002 to +0.029 against a band of 0.085. Version 3: -0.005 (SE 0.008).
  • No theory arm beat plain selection. Version 1: CULTURE +0.105, SOLO +0.102. Version 2: all four theory arms ended below SOLO on the selected task.
  • Social life cut costly capacity in version 1: COOP -1.79, ALPHA -1.11, DOMEST -2.49, MEDLEY -2.08, against a band of 0.98.
  • In version 2 every evolved population ended below the model with no evolved text (0.490 to 0.520 against 0.554). Notes lowered general ability in MEDLEY by 0.027, in 5 of 5 seeds.
  • Training directly on correct answers is the upper bound for any selection scheme. It left general ability unchanged: 0.325 to 0.324 on the 3-family world.

Not tested

  • Theory arms with changing weights. Version 3 built only SOLO and the chance arm.
  • Self-Domestication at full strength. Exile removed 0 agents in version 1 and 4 in version 2, and the grabbing contrast rests on one seed.
  • Large models, runs longer than 12 generations, groups larger than 32.
  • A world broad enough for the ceiling check to pass.

Limits

  • Scale. A frozen Haiku model in versions 1 and 2, and 0.5B and 1.5B open models in version 3. A null result here does not transfer to large models or long runs.
  • Version 1 read ability from two capacity settings through a table measured once, so ability could only move with those settings.
  • Version 2 started below the unmodified model because the starting rule pool held harmful rules. Every gain was recovery toward that model.
  • Version 3 has one adapter per population, and each ceiling arm is one training run. Its standard errors cover test items only.
  • All tests were written for this project by the same author. For the 1.5B model, two of the three general tests sat at chance.

What would change this answer

My recommendation, in order. None of it has been run or priced.

  • Repeat only the ceiling check on a larger open model, with the 16-family world and a public far-transfer test. If direct training cannot move held-out tests, selection cannot either.
  • If that check passes, build the five social arms with changing weights and run all seven.
  • If it fails again, the finding stands as: selection through survival buys task skill, and task skill stays where it was trained.

Follow-up, 2026-11-14: the breadth curve

The first step above was replaced by a cheaper test on the same 1.5B model: 18 runs trained on 2 to 64 task families with a fixed budget. By a rule set in advance the result is flat. Families built on skills the training pool never uses gained +0.067 (SE 0.008) from any training and nothing more from breadth (+0.003 per doubling, SE 0.005). Families from the same pool that a run did not train on did gain with breadth (+0.035 per doubling, SE 0.005), a comparison chosen after seeing the data. That comparison was then rerun with its rule fixed first: 20 new runs, tested on 16 pool families that no run trained on. Confirmed: +0.024 per doubling (SE 0.005), from 0.601 at 2 families to 0.704 at 32 (untrained 0.543), while the held-out families stayed flat in the same runs (+0.003, SE 0.004). Two checks with rules fixed first then repeated it: on a second, separate set of 16 reserved families (+0.026 per doubling, SE 0.005) and on a 3B model with the same families and seeds (+0.024, SE 0.005). Both replicated; the 3B check reran 14 runs after a GPU memory error, a recorded deviation from its plan. Details: breadth curve results. Cost: $2.66 for the first 19 runs, $2.58 for the rerun and $7.89 for the two checks, all on Modal, not included in the table below.

Follow-up, 2026-11-17: the theory arms with changing weights (version 4)

Version 4 ran theory arms with changing weights for the first time. It could not test the thesis. A free check before any model call showed that the social rules could separate an arm from its control by under one doubling of breadth (0.96 at most), where two were needed for a score difference to be detectable with 5 seeds. The verdict was fixed then as not testable at this scale. A smoke test then showed the 1.5B model keeps a shared harvest every time it is asked (15 of 15), so the three self-domestication arms were dropped by rule. Theory 3 is untested again.

25 runs followed: 5 arms, 5 seeds, populations of 32 agents sharing one adapter, 8 generations. Cooperation widened what a population practised by +0.35 doublings (SE 0.10), which is inside the range the hand-written rules produce with no learning (+0.19 to +0.52), so its label is "broadens, mechanical". Copying from successful non-parents changed nothing (-0.02, SE 0.10). Populations did learn: practised skill rose +0.077 (SE 0.014) over the untrained 0.510. By the pre-set rule the credit goes to learning from correct answers and not to selection, because survival selection added +0.001 (SE 0.022) over random parents.

Exploratory, added after the smoke test: all five arms ended above the untrained model on similar unseen tasks, by +0.047 to +0.088 (+0.028 to +0.068 counting valid replies only). Selection against random parents on that score was +0.041 (SE 0.021), just short of the 2 standard error rule. Version 3's learning rule, which copies wrong answers too, scored lower than the new rule on those tasks (-0.036, SE 0.008). Cost $4.53. Version 4 results.
